How far the case went

Each rung is a separate judgment by a separate authority, about a separate object; this page records what each one did and when, citing the act, and never adds them up into a verdict. For Cimbres the central finding is an absence: no formal diocesan ruling from the 1930s–40s has been located. The two documented recent acts are about different objects — the bishop's 2021 recognition of “presumíveis aparições” (presumed apparitions), and the beatification cause of one of the seers, which judges a person and not the events of 1936.

Step by step, with the documents

Local inquiry — the investigators on the ground 1936–1941

✓Investigated — concluded in favour

Fr. José Kehrle (1891–1978), secretary to the Bishop of Pesqueira, and Frei Estevam Roettger OFM (1877–1955), both acting on Bishop Adalberto Accioli Sobral's determination.

The two investigators interrogated the seers repeatedly from August 1936 and attended the reported apparitions in person, pressing for a contradiction; Kehrle separated the girls and put his questions in German and Latin, languages neither could read, on 20 August and after. The diocese's own 2021 pastoral letter records that they returned “o parecer favorável sobre os eventos e o atestado da sua veracidade” — a favourable opinion on the events and an attestation of their truthfulness. Kehrle's 1941 “Manuscripto” is that opinion, and it remains the principal source for every later account. It is an investigator's report and not a ruling: it was never published, Kehrle kept silent under obedience, and no diocesan act adopted it. Two things qualify the rung and are recorded with it. The clergy were not of one mind — Msgr. Elyseu Duarte Diniz, questioning the girls separately on 31 August 1936, declared he believed none of it, and the parish priest Fr. Manuel Marques is reported never to have believed. And the favourable opinion was not the only one the bishop received: Kehrle's diary records that in October 1936 Sobral sent the Franciscan superior and Fr. Urbano de Carvalho to examine the site, that nothing appeared to them, and that they reported the accounts untrue. Neither examination produced a located document. The diary itself is now in print in two devotional editions — Ione Paiva's book and Ana Lígia Lira's “O Inquisidor de Cimbres” (2021), which its author states transcribes it in full — and collating those against the archive copy is the open task.

The bishop's ruling, 1930s–40s 1936–1947 (the episcopate of Dom Adalberto Accioli Sobral)

?A ruling is reported; no document located

Bishop Adalberto Accioli Sobral (1887–1951), second bishop of Pesqueira, who administered the diocese from 1934 to 1947 and who ordered the inquiry.

Devotional literature reports that the Church answered in this period by rejecting the reports and imposing silence — the Heralds of the Gospel add police pressure and some fifty years of enforced silence on the seers and on Kehrle — but it produces no decree, and that characterization is its own. What better sources establish is narrower and is not a ruling: the 2023 academic study finds the cult was legitimated by its devotees “without major institutional interference from the local Curia or the Roman Curia” and describes strategies of silencing rather than an act, while the diocese's 2021 pastoral letter records “silenciamento das autoridades eclesiásticas” and the detention of Maria da Luz's father by police in 1936, then calls itself “a primeira resposta da Igreja” — the Church's first response to these events. Four episcopal actions of the period are now dated and attested, none of them a ruling: the October 1936 second examination that reported against the accounts; the police action of 20 October 1936 that destroyed the enclosure at the spring and detained Artur Teixeira, ordered in the name of the regional delegado of Pesqueira, which the town attributed to the bishop and which the bishop had Kehrle deny from the cathedral pulpit; the bishop's rebuke to Kehrle for paying a seer's school fees instead of a seminarian's; and a letter to Kehrle in 1937, quoted in the devotional edition of his papers, which says nothing about the apparitions and everything about obedience — whoever is not with the bishop is not with the Church. A private letter about authority is not a judgment on a case. So a ruling is reported and no document has been located. That is not the same as a ruling against, and this page does not record one.

No formal diocesan decree on the apparitions, favourable or adverse, has been located for the 1930s–40s. Searched, August 2026: the 2023 RBHR study, which works from Kehrle's 1941 manuscript and cites no such act; the 2016 UNICAP dissertation, which retells the whole 1936–37 sequence from that diary and likewise cites none; the 2021 pastoral letter, which reviews the history and cites none either; Catholic press; the shrine's own history; the devotional literature that asserts the rejection; and eighteen devotional videos supplied for this project in 2026, in which every speaker asserts an episcopal prohibition and not one names a decree, a protocol number or an archival reference. The 2016 dissertation adds a checked zero of its own: it found no coverage of the events in the Jornal do Commercio or the Diário de Pernambuco for August–November 1936. Three archives would settle it and none has been consulted here — the diocesan archive of Pesqueira, which holds Kehrle's manuscript and is not online; the papers of the Pesqueira police delegacy and the Pernambuco chefia de polícia, where an order for the October 1936 detention would be; and the Irmã Adélia collection donated to the Instituto RIC in Recife in July 2023.

The bishop's judgment, 2021 — the pastoral letter 2 October 2021

✓Investigated — concluded in favour

Bishop José Luiz Ferreira Salles CSsR of Pesqueira, signing at Aldeia Guarda, Vila de Cimbres.

The letter is titled “A Diocese de Pesqueira e as presumíveis aparições de Nossa Senhora da Graça” — presumed apparitions — and the word does work all through it. Acting “pela autoridade que nos confere o nosso ministério episcopal”, the bishop declares that in the reported apparitions there are “elementos, traços e sinais que abalizam e sinalizam a grande probabilidade do caráter sobrenatural” of what the two girls lived; that the messages are “em profunda consonância com as verdades da fé cristã”; that this finding of great probability, while making the events worthy of credence, “não vincula ou obriga nenhum fiel a crer”; and that he grants permission for the faithful to continue the devotion. Official interpretation is reserved to the Magisterium and to the diocesan bishop, and the letter says expressly that it does not exhaust the possibility of later decisions by the competent ecclesial authorities. It is a diocesan act finding a high probability and permitting a cult — not a declaration that the apparitions are established, and not a papal act.

Referral to Rome on the apparitions

·No record found that this step took place

The Holy See. The 2021 pastoral letter proceeds under the Holy See's 1978 norms for discerning presumed apparitions and reserves official pronouncements on the case to the Magisterium and to the diocesan bishop of Pesqueira.

Nothing found indicates that the apparitions were ever referred to Rome or ruled on there. Searched, August 2026: the 2023 RBHR study, which concludes the cult consolidated “without major institutional interference from the local Curia or the Roman Curia”; the 2021 pastoral letter, which reviews the case at length, cites the Roman norms, reports no Roman act on it and calls itself the Church's first response; Catholic press, which presents the 2021 act as diocesan; and the Latin title variants for a curial document, logged as a verified zero in this project's KEYWORDS.md. This is recorded as “no record found” — a statement about the evidence reached — and deliberately not as “the case did not reach this step”, which would be a claim about the case itself. Settling that would take the registers of the competent dicastery, which this project has not seen; a study's finding of no interference is compatible with a Roman act that did not interfere. One further test was run in 2026 and it came back the same way. Asked on camera, in May 2021, whether the Holy See had a file on Cimbres, Ana Lígia Lira — who has worked longer in these documents than anyone outside the cause — answered that it is said so “nos bastidores”, and immediately added “mas eu não tenho provas disso, não tenho dados disso”. The tradition's own researcher reports the rumour and disclaims the evidence, which is what this rung already says.

Rome — the beatification cause of Irmã Adélia, a different object Nihil obstat 22–23 February 2024; diocesan phase 10 March – 13 October 2024; Roman phase from 17 October 2024

◆Church act on a related matter — not a ruling on the apparition

The Dicastery for the Causes of Saints, and the Diocese of Pesqueira for the diocesan phase.

This rung is about a person, not about the 1936 events. The Dicastery's nihil obstat opened the beatification and canonization cause of Irmã Adélia — Maria da Luz Teixeira de Carvalho, the younger of the two seers — which gives her the title Servant of God; Catholic press differs over whether the act fell on 22 or 23 February 2024. The diocesan phase opened on 10 March 2024 and closed on 13 October 2024, and the acts went to Rome, where the cause is under way. A cause examines a candidate's life and virtue: nothing in it is a judgment on the apparitions, and headlines of the form “the Vatican recognizes the apparition of Cimbres” misreport it. The 2021 pastoral letter had already announced that the nihil obstat was requested and awaited, treating the cause as a matter distinct from the events. The rung is here because it is the only Roman act in this file, and omitting it would leave that confusion unanswered. The diocesan acts were delivered to the Dicastery on 17 October 2024 by a delegation received in the papal audience the previous day: the congregation's superior general and provincial, the postulator Frei Jociel Gomes, and Prof. Dr. Carlos André Moura as president of the cause's historical and archival commission — the same historian who wrote the 2023 academic study this dataset leans on most, which is worth a reader's knowing. No miracle has been recognised in the cause, and none of the cures reported at Cimbres has been the object of any Church declaration.

About

Work-in-progress dataset compiled from public sources (see references). The apparitions are recorded here as REPORTED events: the dataset documents who reported what and when, and what Church authorities ruled and when — it does not assert the supernatural claim as fact. The same rule governs the cures reported at the site: the datable thing is that a cure was REPORTED, by whom and when, or that an authority RECOGNISED one — and at Cimbres no recognition of any cure has been located, so no cure is recorded here as having occurred. The scope runs from the colonial founding of Cimbres, through the 1936–37 reports and the diocesan response, to the shrine, the seers' later lives and the beatification cause now in Rome; Xukuru history is cited only where a dated event here requires it and is not this project's subject. Sourcing is thin: much of the literature is devotional and recycles itself, several dates rest on a single devotional source and are flagged with a ? mark, and where a devotional book or video is the only witness to something the dataset names the witness. Contested claims are attributed to their authors. Corrections against primary sources are welcome.

What it is
A series of Marian apparitions reported in 1936 (and, per some accounts, into 1937) by two adolescent girls at Sítio Guarda, near the village of Cimbres, municipality of Pesqueira, Pernambuco, Brazil, attributed by the devotion to Nossa Senhora das Graças (Our Lady of Grace).[1] [2]
Reported seers
Maria da Conceição Silva (1920–1999), then 16, and Maria da Luz Teixeira de Carvalho (1922–2013), then 13 — the latter later a religious sister under the name Irmã Adélia.[1] [6]
Church status
No formal diocesan ruling from the 1930s–40s has been located: a 2023 academic study concludes the devotion consolidated 'without major institutional interference from the local Curia or the Roman Curia', while devotional literature reports informal dismissal and imposed silence. The first official pronouncement came on 2 October 2021, when Bishop José Luiz Ferreira Salles of Pesqueira published a pastoral letter recognizing the 'presumíveis aparições' (presumed apparitions), stating they show 'great probability of supernatural character', and permitting the devotion. This is a diocesan act, not a papal one.[1] [4] [10]
The site today
A diocesan shrine — the Santuário de Nossa Senhora das Graças at Aldeia Guarda, Vila de Cimbres — with an annual pilgrimage feast on 31 August and a 365-step stairway up to the rock. The village and shrine lie inside the demarcated Xukuru Indigenous Land on the Serra do Ororubá. The ninetieth anniversary of the first reported apparition fell on 6 August 2026.[8] [12] [26]
Reported cures
Cures and wonders are reported at the site and attributed to Nossa Senhora das Graças, and pilgrims come for the water of the spring. None has been recognised by any Church authority: no bishop's or dicastery's declaration concerning a cure at Cimbres has been located, and the 2021 pastoral letter makes none. The earliest cure testimony that the 2016 UNICAP study can date is from the pilgrimage of 31 August 1985, and that study states it has no testimonies from the period of the apparitions themselves; Kehrle's diary claims a register kept at the Guarda in 1936 already held some six hundred, and that register has not been produced. This dataset records who reported what and when. It makes no finding that any cure occurred.[15] [3] [19]

1676 1676 ? Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil An Oratorian mission is founded on the Serra do Ororubá — Fr. João Duarte do Sacramento of the Congregation of the Oratory founds a mission among the Ararobá on the Serra do Ororubá and names it for Nossa Senhora das Montanhas — Our Lady of the Mountains. 1762 1762-04-03 Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil Cimbres is raised to a vila — The settlement is elevated to the status of vila under the name Cimbres, the sixth in Pernambuco, with a term covering territory now divided among more than twenty municipalities. Pesqueira, in the valley below, is made a vila on 13 May 1836 and takes the municipal seat from Cimbres; from 1913 the municipality is definitively named Pesqueira and Cimbres is one of its districts. By 1936 Cimbres is a village that had once been the head of the region and no longer was.[28]
1910s
1910 1910-12-05 Floresta, Pernambuco, Brazil The Diocese of Floresta is erected — The diocese that will judge the Cimbres reports is erected as the Diocese of Floresta, in the Pernambuco sertão; its see moves to Pesqueira and it is renamed on 2 August 1918. It is a young, poor and thinly staffed diocese: in 1936 it is twenty-six years old, and the priest who conducts the inquiry is the bishop's secretary rather than a canonist or a commission.[11]
1918 1918-08-02 Pesqueira, Pernambuco, Brazil The episcopal see is transferred to Pesqueira — The Diocese of Floresta (created 1910) is transferred to Pesqueira, taking in the parishes of Pesqueira and Cimbres among others; the Diocese of Pesqueira is the ecclesiastical authority over all later events in this chronology.[1] [11]
1930s
1934 1934-01-13 Pesqueira, Pernambuco, Brazil Adalberto Accioli Sobral is appointed bishop, ending a fourteen-month vacancy — After the death of Bishop José Antônio de Oliveira Lopes on 24 November 1932 the see stands vacant for fourteen months. Adalberto Accioli Sobral is appointed on 13 January 1934 and governs Pesqueira until his transfer on 18 January 1947. The reported apparitions fall two and a half years into his episcopate, and every ecclesiastical act and non-act of the 1936–37 period is his.[11]
1936 1936-08-06 Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil First reported apparition — Maria da Conceição Silva (16) and Maria da Luz Teixeira de Carvalho (13) report seeing a woman with a child on a rock of the Serra do Ororubá while gathering castor beans; in later reported dialogues the figure, asked who she was, answers 'Eu sou a graça' ('I am the grace'). Pilgrims begin arriving the next day, per the shrine's account.[1] [4] [8]
1936 1936-08-15 ? Pesqueira, Brazil The Diocese of Pesqueira opens an inquiry — After the reports reach the diocese, Bishop Adalberto Accioli Sobral designates his secretary, Fr. José Kehrle, to investigate the events and draft an opinion (parecer) on the credibility of the accounts, following canonical procedure. Devotional transcriptions date Kehrle's first recorded interrogation of Maria da Luz to 15 August 1936.[1] [9]
1936 1936-08-20 Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil Kehrle examines the seers on the mountain — Fr. José Kehrle goes to Sítio Guarda in person and conducts the first on-site examination, having already sent up a written note of questions built on an exorcism formula ('Quem pode mais que Deus?') because, on his own account, he feared a diabolical event. He separates the girls, questions them apart, and puts his questions in German and Latin — languages neither could read — with the answers returned in Portuguese; the accounts give some 123 questions on this day. Kehrle reported seeing nothing himself. This is the method the whole case is argued from, by its defenders and against its doubters alike.[15] [1] [19]
1936 1936-08-31 Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil Mass gathering at the site; clergy divided — On a date the seers said the apparition itself had appointed, a crowd gathers at the site — the shrine says more than 5,000 pilgrims, Kehrle's account as narrated in the devotional literature says more than 3,000. Kehrle attends with his brother, Fr. Luiz Kehrle, and with Msgr. Elyseu Duarte Diniz; his diary records the message 'Brazil is saved, communism is not coming any more'. The same day Diniz, after questioning the girls separately, declares he believes none of it, an early sign of the split Kehrle's diary describes among clergy and faithful. Ione Paiva, the tradition's principal narrator, says in a 2025 interview that the word 'communism' enters the record through Kehrle's questions rather than through the reported messages; readers of the political material should weigh that.[1] [8] [15] [29]
1936 1936-09-10 Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil The parish priest and the Franciscan question the seers; a doctor is called in — Fr. Manuel Marques, parish priest of Cimbres, and Frei Estevão von Roettger OFM question the seers on the mountain in Latin and German. Marques then tells Artur Teixeira in front of witnesses to take the girls to Pesqueira to be examined by a physician; Dr. Lídio Paraíba examines both and certifies in writing that their physical and mental condition showed no abnormality. The medical certificate is the one piece of non-clerical documentation the 1936 inquiry is reported to have produced, and it has not been located.[15] [17]
1936 1936-10 ? Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil A second examination, commissioned by the bishop, reports against the reports — Kehrle's diary records that in October Bishop Sobral sent the Franciscan superior together with Fr. Urbano de Carvalho, rector of the college, to examine the events on the spot. They climbed the mountain with the girls, nothing appeared, they concluded the accounts were untrue and reported so to the bishop. This matters for how the inquiry is described: the diocese did not receive one favourable opinion, it received a favourable one from its secretary and an adverse one from two examiners it sent afterwards. No document from either examination has been located; both are known only through Kehrle's diary as printed in the devotional literature. The month is Kehrle's; no day is given.[15] [19]
1936 1936-10-20 Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil Police destroy the enclosure at the spring and detain the seer's father — A police detachment under a sergeant, acting in the name of the regional delegado of Pesqueira, destroys the fence and door built around the spring and places Artur Teixeira — Maria da Luz's father and the owner of the land — under arrest, to be taken to Pesqueira and from there to the chief of police in Recife. He is questioned by the regional delegado, found not to be at fault, and released the same night. This is the detention the diocese's own 2021 pastoral letter recalls; Kehrle's diary supplies the date and the sequence. Who ordered it was disputed at the time: the town said the bishop, and Kehrle was instructed by the bishop to say from the cathedral pulpit that this was a rumour. The order, if written, would be in the papers of the Pesqueira police delegacy or of the Pernambuco chefia de polícia, which this project has not seen.[15] [3] [19]
1937 1937-02-26 ? Sítio Guarda, Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil The last reported apparition at Sítio Guarda — and the accounts diverge on it — The 2016 UNICAP study, following Kehrle's diary, dates the last reported appearance at Sítio Guarda to 26 February 1937, with a written message that ends 'Eu aparecerei em outro monte' ('I shall appear on another mountain'); reports then shift to a place called Alagadiço near Alagoinha, still in the Diocese of Pesqueira, and Kehrle sends questions there through Maria da Luz. The devotional accounts do not agree with each other: the narrated video series dates the end to 26 December 1936, Ione Paiva says February 1937 with about forty apparitions in all, and the shrine counts thirty-nine after the first. A date of 11 February 1937, which circulates in search results, was not found in the text of any source reached. The dataset records the divergence rather than choosing among them.[15] [19] [29] [32]
1937 1937-06 ? Pesqueira, Brazil Kehrle consults Germany, and both investigators are moved out of Pesqueira — Uncertain whether the events were of God, Kehrle writes to Friedrich Ritter von Lama in Germany — the writer and confidant of the stigmatic Therese Neumann — asking him to put the question to her; the reply reaches him in June 1937 and is reported as favourable. He forwards it to the bishop and receives back a letter that answers nothing about the apparitions and everything about authority: those who oppose the bishop 'não poderão contar vitória', for whoever is not with the bishop is not with the Church. Around the same time Kehrle and Frei Estevão are notified of their transfers out of Pesqueira. The bishop's letter is quoted in the devotional edition of Kehrle's papers and is the nearest thing to a written episcopal position on the case located for this period — and it is a private letter about obedience, not a ruling on the reports. The Kehrle–von Lama correspondence is a documentary lead that has not been traced to an archive.[15] [19]
1940s
1940 1940-06-12 ? Recife, Pernambuco, Brazil Maria da Luz enters religious life — Maria da Luz is presented by Sra. Basinha Galindo and enters the postulancy of the Religiosas da Instrução Cristã (Damas da Instrução Cristã) at Recife; the shrine's biography dates the entry to 12 June 1940, the start of the noviciate to 14 January 1941, and the temporary vows at which she took the name Irmã Adélia to 14 January 1943, while Catholic press gives her perpetual profession as 1946. She had asked Kehrle beforehand to impose on her, as a penance, silence about the apparitions — she feared the congregation would not accept a seer — and she kept it for forty-nine years. Her fellow seer Maria da Conceição remained a laywoman. The formation dates rest on the shrine's account alone; the earlier reading of this entry, 'December 1940', came from a different devotional source.[25] [9] [6] [1] [29]
1941 1941 Pesqueira, Brazil Kehrle's favorable report is completed — and no ruling follows — Fr. José Kehrle completes his 'Manuscripto', a diary-report concluding favorably on the reported apparitions; it becomes the principal source for later study. No formal diocesan judgment is documented: the 2023 academic study of the case concludes the cult was legitimated by devotees 'without major institutional interference from the local Curia or the Roman Curia', and describes strategies of silencing by the hierarchy — Kehrle kept his opinion unpublished under obedience. Devotional literature (Heralds of the Gospel) goes further, reporting a campaign of suppression that included police pressure and roughly fifty years of imposed silence on the seers and on Kehrle; that characterization is theirs. The manuscript itself has never been published as such, but two devotional editions now carry it in print: Ione Paiva's book, which reproduces its material alongside Irmã Adélia's own writings, and Ana Lígia Lira's 'O Inquisidor de Cimbres' (2021), which its author states is a full transcription of the diary together with Maria da Luz's letters. Collating those printings against the archive copy is the outstanding task on this case.[1] [10] [19] [16]
1942 1942 ? Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil An image of Nossa Senhora das Graças is enthroned in the chapel — Per the shrine's own history, an image of Nossa Senhora das Graças donated by Odete Berardo is enthroned in the small chapel that Arthur Teixeira de Carvalho, Maria da Luz's father, had built near the site. Single devotional source; date unverified elsewhere.[8]
1970s
1978 1978-08-06 Buíque, Pernambuco, Brazil Fr. José Kehrle dies at Buíque — Kehrle — born at Rheinstetten, Germany, on 19 May 1891, in Brazil from 1909, ordained in 1914, made a monsignor in 1964 — had been moved to Buíque in 1947, where he served as priest and unpaid physician and built a chapel, a school and a maternity hospital, all under the title Nossa Senhora das Graças. He dies there on 6 August 1978, the anniversary of the first reported apparition, and is buried in the chapel he built. He never published the report he wrote in 1941; the coincidence of the date is a staple of the devotional accounts and is recorded here as a date, not as a sign.[23] [1] [15]
1980s
1985 1985 Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil Irmã Adélia breaks a forty-nine-year silence — Diagnosed with breast cancer in 1984 and, on the accounts of those around her, given a short prognosis after metastasis, Irmã Adélia asks her superior's leave to tell her community about 1936 and then joins a congregational pilgrimage to Sítio Guarda — her first return. She abandons treatment afterwards and lives a further twenty-eight years. That she was cured is what the congregation, her companion Irmã Viviane and her secretary Ione Paiva report, and what the Catholic press repeats; it is recorded here as a report, with its reporters, and no Church act has declared it. From this point organised pilgrimages of hundreds begin arriving, the first from Fortaleza brought by Frei Marcos Queiroz Câmara, and the case enters public circulation for the first time since 1937.[6] [8] [25] [29] [15]
1985 1985-08-31 ? Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil The earliest dated report of a cure attributed to the site — The earliest cure account the 2016 UNICAP study can date is Maria Eunice do Rego Barros's, from the pilgrimage of 31 August 1985; the study states in terms that it has no testimonies from the period of the apparitions themselves and that the reports begin here. Further accounts it collects are dated 1986 to 1989 and are attributed to named individuals. Against that stands Kehrle's own diary, which claims that a register kept at the Guarda in 1936 already held some six hundred testimonies — a register nobody in the reachable literature has produced. What is recorded here is that cures were reported, by whom and when: this dataset makes no finding that any cure occurred, and no bishop or dicastery has recognised one at Cimbres.[15] [19]
1986 1986 ? Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil A reported episcopal statement at the fiftieth anniversary — Reporting on Pesqueira politics in 2020, Brasil de Fato states that at the 1986 jubilee of the reported vision Bishop Manuel Palmeira da Rocha 'admitiu o testemunho das duas crianças como verdadeiro'. No text of any such statement has been located, and the diocese's 2021 pastoral letter — which reviews the whole history — presents itself as the Church's first response. The claim is recorded as reported, with its reporter and its date; it is not treated as a Church act.[27] [3]
1988 1980–1993 ? Cimbres, Pesqueira, Brazil The site is decreed a sanctuary — under a bishop whose dates do not fit the reported year — The shrine's own history states that Bishop Manoel Palmeira da Rocha of Pesqueira decreed the site the Santuário de Nossa Senhora das Graças on 20 October 1998, and the regional press repeats it. He cannot have: Palmeira da Rocha governed Pesqueira from 14 March 1980 until 26 May 1993, and the bishop in 1998 was Bernardino Marchiò. Ione Paiva, who was present, says instead that she and Irmã Adélia received the sanctuary licence from Palmeira da Rocha in 1988 and that his signed decree is reproduced in her book. Either the year or the bishop in the received account is wrong. This dataset records the act as falling in Palmeira da Rocha's episcopate, leaves the date unresolved, and notes that the decree text has not been reached — a copy is said to be printed in a book that is not sold.[8] [11] [30] [26]
1990s
1999 1999-01-17 ? Arcoverde, Pernambuco, Brazil Maria da Conceição Silva dies at Arcoverde — The elder of the two seers — born at Sanharó on 23 February 1920 to José Manoel da Silva and Jesuína Maria da Conceição, and living with the Teixeira de Carvalho family from about the age of thirteen — dies at Arcoverde and is buried in the Cemitério São Cristóvão there. She stopped reporting the apparitions during 1936, returned to her family, was married by her father in a marriage that did not last, and lived in poverty; the shrine states that no beatification cause has been opened for her. The date rests on the shrine's biography alone and other devotional sources give 22 January; her life after 1936 is the thinnest-documented part of this record.[24] [31] [1]
2000s
2001 2001-04-30 Pesqueira, Brazil The Xukuru Indigenous Land, which contains Cimbres, is homologated — The Brazilian presidency homologates the demarcation of the 27,555-hectare Terra Indígena Xukuru on the Serra do Ororubá (decree published 2 May 2001). Vila de Cimbres and the sanctuary lie within this territory — the shrine's later history unfolds on demarcated Xukuru land, whose people also keep an older Marian devotion of their own, 'Mãe Tamain' (Nossa Senhora das Montanhas).[12] [13] [1]

Irmã Adélia (Maria da Luz Teixeira de Carvalho)

23 February 1920 – 17 January 1999 · Brazil

Reported seer, aged 16 in 1936; born at Sanharó and living with the Teixeira de Carvalho family; stopped reporting the apparitions during 1936 and returned to Arcoverde, where she lived in poverty and died. Fr. José Kehrle

Disambiguation & nuance

An obscure, mostly devotional literature surrounds this subject; the distinctions below are the ones most often flattened in it.

Approved for pilgrimage is not the same as approved as an apparition — and that is the Church's rule, not this page's

A shrine, a crowned image, a feast, indulgences, a papal visit: it is reasonable to read all that as the Church saying the apparition happened. It is not what the Church says it is doing, and since 2024 it says so in writing. The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith's "Norms for Proceeding in the Discernment of Alleged Supernatural Phenomena" (17 May 2024) sets out six possible conclusions, and art. 23 makes the position explicit: "as a rule, neither the Diocesan Bishop, nor the Episcopal Conferences, nor the Dicastery will declare that these phenomena are of supernatural origin", though the Pope may authorise a special procedure. The ordinary favourable outcome is now "Nihil obstat", which art. 17 defines as acknowledging "many signs of the action of the Holy Spirit" in a spiritual experience "without expressing any certainty about the supernatural authenticity of the phenomenon itself". The document's own presentation adds that most shrines "have never had an official declaration of the supernatural nature of the events". The reason is what a private revelation is. Public revelation closed with the apostles, so no apparition adds an article of faith, and even an approved one obliges no one to believe — the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith said exactly that when it published the third part of the Fátima secret in 2000. What a bishop is actually asked is a pastoral question: is this devotion safe, is it free of doctrinal error, is it bearing good fruit? That can be answered yes without answering the historical question, and usually is. The same acts are granted to devotions with no apparition behind them at all — Our Lady of the Rosary and Our Lady of Mount Carmel have feasts, crowned images and basilicas, and there is no reported apparition to approve. So this page is not withholding approval; it is saying who gave which one, about what. Where a bishop did rule on an apparition, the ladder says so in his own words and marks the rung favourable — Lourdes 1862 and La Salette 1851 are quoted verbatim, Fátima 1930 is cited to its pastoral letter. What the ladder refuses to do is upgrade a crown into a verdict. If it did, a case a bishop examined and endorsed would look identical to a case nobody ever ruled on, because both have pilgrims; and the reverse error would follow just as easily, reading a Roman act against one seer's later writings as Rome condemning the apparition. Keeping the objects apart is the only way the chart carries information at all. Where the sources themselves say the Church's recognition is "implicit", the page records that word too, and does not promote it.[33]

Cimbres 1936 ≠ the Miraculous Medal (Paris, 1830)

'Nossa Senhora das Graças' is also the Brazilian title of Our Lady of Graces of the Miraculous Medal (Rue du Bac, Paris, 1830). The Cimbres devotion uses the same title — per the inquiry records because the reported figure answered 'Eu sou a graça' — but it is a distinct reported apparition with its own history.[1]

The 2021 'recognition' is diocesan, not a Vatican approval of the apparitions

The 2 October 2021 act is a pastoral letter of the Bishop of Pesqueira on 'presumed apparitions' asserting 'great probability' of supernatural character. The 2024 Vatican act (the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints' nihil obstat) concerns Irmã Adélia's beatification cause, not the apparitions — despite press headlines such as 'Vatican recognizes apparition'.[4] [7]

Documented silence ≠ a published condemnation

Devotional tradition speaks of early Church rejection of Cimbres. What the sources actually show is narrower: no formal decree — negative or positive — has been located for the 1930s–40s; the academic study documents informal dismissal, non-publication of Kehrle's favorable report, and strategies of silencing, and devotional literature adds (on its own authority) police pressure and decades of imposed silence. This dataset records that absence rather than inventing a ruling.[1] [10]

Mãe Tamain ≠ Nossa Senhora das Graças de Cimbres

The Xukuru devotion to Nossa Senhora das Montanhas, called 'Mãe Tamain', predates 1936 and is the patroness of the local parish; it is distinct from the 1936 apparition devotion, though both share the same mountain.[1] [12]

A reported cure is not a recognised miracle

Cures are reported at Cimbres in quantity, and they are told in the same breath as the bishop's acts. They are not the same kind of thing. A cure enters this dataset only as a dated report with a named reporter, or as a Church act recognising it — and at Cimbres there is no such act, for any cure, by any authority. The 1985 recovery of Irmã Adélia is the case in point: it is central to the devotion, it is what her congregation and her secretary report, and it has never been the object of a declaration. Cures are also deliberately absent from the approval ladder, which is about judgments on the apparitions.[15] [6] [3]

The sanctuary decree's usual date cannot be right as told

Almost every account, the shrine's own included, dates the sanctuary decree to 20 October 1998 and attributes it to Bishop Manoel Palmeira da Rocha. His episcopate ran from 1980 to 26 May 1993. Ione Paiva, who says she received the licence with Irmã Adélia and that the signed decree is printed in her book, gives 1988. Something in the received account is wrong, and the dataset says so rather than repeating it.[8] [11] [30]

The books behind the videos, and why they matter more than the videos

The devotional video material on Cimbres is largely a narration of four books: Ione Paiva's 'Aqui o Céu encontra-se com a Terra' (1987/1990, 10th ed. 2024), Ana Lígia Lira's 'O Diário do Silêncio' (2018) and 'O Inquisidor de Cimbres' (2021), and Dom Rafael Maria Francisco da Silva OSB's 'Eu sou a Graça' (2016) — with, behind all of them, Fr. Kehrle's diary and Frei Estevão's. The books are devotional too, and two of the four are by participants; but they are citable, paginated and checkable in a way a video is not, and Lira's 2021 volume claims to print the diary in full. Where this dataset repeats something the videos say, it says who said it.[19] [16] [17] [18]
