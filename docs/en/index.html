How far the case went

Each rung is a separate judgment by a separate authority, about a separate object; this page records what each one did and when, citing the act, and never adds them up into a verdict. For Cimbres the central finding is an absence: no formal diocesan ruling from the 1930s–40s has been located. The two documented recent acts are about different objects — the bishop's 2021 recognition of “presumíveis aparições” (presumed apparitions), and the beatification cause of one of the seers, which judges a person and not the events of 1936.

Sourcing for this subject is thin: much of the literature is devotional, and several dates rest on a single devotional source; those are flagged with a ? mark. What it is
A series of Marian apparitions reported in 1936 (and, per some accounts, into 1937) by two adolescent girls at Sítio Guarda, near the village of Cimbres, municipality of Pesqueira, Pernambuco, Brazil, attributed by the devotion to Nossa Senhora das Graças (Our Lady of Grace).[1] [2]
Reported seers
Maria da Conceição Silva (1920–1999), then 16, and Maria da Luz Teixeira de Carvalho (1922–2013), then 13 — the latter later a religious sister under the name Irmã Adélia.[1] [6]
Church status
No formal diocesan ruling from the 1930s–40s has been located: a 2023 academic study concludes the devotion consolidated 'without major institutional interference from the local Curia or the Roman Curia', while devotional literature reports informal dismissal and imposed silence. The first official pronouncement came on 2 October 2021, when Bishop José Luiz Ferreira Salles of Pesqueira published a pastoral letter recognizing the 'presumíveis aparições' (presumed apparitions), stating they show 'great probability of supernatural character', and permitting the devotion. This is a diocesan act, not a papal one.[1] [4] [10]
The site today
A diocesan shrine — the Santuário de Nossa Senhora das Graças at Aldeia Guarda, Vila de Cimbres — with an annual pilgrimage feast on 31 August. The village and shrine lie inside the demarcated Xukuru Indigenous Land on the Serra do Ororubá.[8] [12]

Disambiguation & nuance

An obscure, mostly devotional literature surrounds this subject; the distinctions below are the ones most often flattened in it.

Cimbres 1936 ≠ the Miraculous Medal (Paris, 1830)

'Nossa Senhora das Graças' is also the Brazilian title of Our Lady of Graces of the Miraculous Medal (Rue du Bac, Paris, 1830). The Cimbres devotion uses the same title — per the inquiry records because the reported figure answered 'Eu sou a graça' — but it is a distinct reported apparition with its own history.[1]

The 2021 'recognition' is diocesan, not a Vatican approval of the apparitions

The 2 October 2021 act is a pastoral letter of the Bishop of Pesqueira on 'presumed apparitions' asserting 'great probability' of supernatural character. The 2024 Vatican act (the Dicastery for the Causes of Saints' nihil obstat) concerns Irmã Adélia's beatification cause, not the apparitions — despite press headlines such as 'Vatican recognizes apparition'.[4] [7]

Documented silence ≠ a published condemnation

Devotional tradition speaks of early Church rejection of Cimbres. What the sources actually show is narrower: no formal decree — negative or positive — has been located for the 1930s–40s; the academic study documents informal dismissal, non-publication of Kehrle's favorable report, and strategies of silencing, and devotional literature adds (on its own authority) police pressure and decades of imposed silence. This dataset records that absence rather than inventing a ruling.[1] [10]

Mãe Tamain ≠ Nossa Senhora das Graças de Cimbres

The Xukuru devotion to Nossa Senhora das Montanhas, called 'Mãe Tamain', predates 1936 and is the patroness of the local parish; it is distinct from the 1936 apparition devotion, though both share the same mountain.[1] [12]
